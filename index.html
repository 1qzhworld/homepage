Biography

He received his Master's degree in Communication and Information Systems from the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, China, under the supervision of Prof. Jie Lu in 2020. Following his Master's studies, he joined Hithink Royal Flush Info. Network Co. as an algorithm developer, focusing on federated learning algorithm development, before leaving in 2021. He obtained his B.S. degree in Electronic Information Science and Technology from Hefei University of Technology, Anhui, China, in 2017. His research interests include, but are not limited to, developing algorithms for distributed optimization, federated learning, and online optimization problems, with a particular focus on the security issues therein.

Zhihai has a strong reputation among colleagues from diverse perspectives. However, he is not without flaws; sometimes, he spends too much time on a single area in his quest to satisfy his curiosity. He found that pursuing research as a career often brought more pressure than inspiration, and hopes that this new path will allow him to explore meaningful and practical ways to make a positive impact on the world. Whether he returns to academia one day remains uncertain—but his enthusiasm for impactful research remains unchanged.

Current Role: In September 2026 he left BaoSight Software and joined X-Times Design Automation Co., Ltd. (XTIMES-DA), a company devoted to digital-implementation EDA, where he is currently an engineer in the Placement R&D Department.

The Focus: Placement and routing (P&R) in the physical design of EDA—deciding where each cell of a digital chip should be placed and how the wires between them should be routed, so that timing, congestion, and design-rule constraints are satisfied at the same time.

A Short Introduction to EDA: Electronic Design Automation (EDA) is the software stack that takes a chip from a description of its behavior to a manufacturable layout. Starting from a register-transfer-level (RTL) design, it plans the floorplan, places millions of standard cells and macros, builds the clock tree, routes billions of wires, and checks timing, power, and design rules—a job that is far beyond manual effort and must be repeated many times before a single chip can be taped out. Digital implementation is the part of the flow that turns logic into geometry, and placement is its first and most consequential step, since it fixes the skeleton of the layout and bounds what the later steps (clock-tree synthesis, routing, timing closure) can still repair. The underlying problems are among the largest combinatorial optimization problems in industry, and the algorithms that solve them combine mathematical optimization, heuristic search, and, increasingly, machine learning.

The Appeal: It is one of the hardest combinatorial optimization problems in the semiconductor industry, and it lets him keep doing what he has always enjoyed—turning optimization theory into algorithms that people actually use under real production pressures of runtime and quality of results. In his view, an optimization algorithm should survive a real chip, which is the ultimate test: the silicon always gives the honest answer.

In his free time, he explores the practical side of LLM development, experimenting with various frameworks and implementation techniques to uncover new possibilities. He is eager to navigate the emerging 'Agent Age', where he focuses on the potential of multi-agent architectures and how they can be leveraged to solve complex, collaborative tasks.
